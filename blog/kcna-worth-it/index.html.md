# Is KCNA worth $250 as a first certification

> KCNA is multiple choice, has no prerequisite, and is the cheapest cert in the Linux Foundation catalog. Here's what it actually covers, and who should skip it entirely.

Published: 2026-08-24 · Updated: 2026-08-24 · Tags: kubernetes, certification
Canonical: https://rushabhshah.dev/blog/kcna-worth-it/

KCNA (Kubernetes and Cloud Native Associate) is the cheapest certification in the whole Linux Foundation and CNCF catalog at $250 list, and it's the one I get asked about most by people who are new to cloud native, usually with some version of "is this worth it or should I just go straight for CKA."

## What KCNA actually covers

It's 90 minutes, entirely multiple choice, and there's no live cluster or terminal involved. The domains are Kubernetes fundamentals, container orchestration, cloud native architecture, cloud native observability, and cloud native application delivery. There's no certification prerequisite to sit it.

In practice, that means KCNA tests whether you know what the pieces are and how they fit together: what a Pod is versus a Deployment, what a Service does, what GitOps means, what observability is and why it matters, that kind of landscape literacy. It doesn't test whether you can actually operate a cluster under time pressure. That's what CKA is for.

## Cost versus the alternatives

$250 for a multiple-choice exam is a fair question to interrogate, because free options exist. The Linux Foundation and CNCF publish free introductory Kubernetes course material covering a lot of the same ground, and there's no shortage of free YouTube courses and blog series on the same fundamentals. If your only goal is learning the material, you don't strictly need to pay $250 to do that.

What the $250 buys you is a CNCF-recognized credential you can put on a resume or LinkedIn profile that a hiring manager or recruiter can independently verify, which self-study alone doesn't give you. Whether that's worth it to you depends entirely on whether you're job-hunting or career-switching right now, versus just learning for your own use.

## Who it's genuinely useful for

Students, career-changers, and anyone moving into cloud native from another field with no Kubernetes background yet are the exact audience this was built for. It's a real, verifiable credential at a price that doesn't gate people out, and the vocabulary you build studying for it removes a lot of friction when you move on to CKA later, since you're not learning what a Pod is *and* trying to move fast in a live terminal at the same time.

## Who should skip straight to CKA

If you're already working with Kubernetes day to day, running clusters, debugging deployments, reading manifests without needing a glossary, KCNA tests things you already know, and $250 is better spent going straight at CKA, which is the credential that actually gets checked for production-facing roles. KCNA isn't a required stepping stone for anyone; it's an on-ramp for people who genuinely need one.

## Verdict

Worth it if you're new to the ecosystem and want a verifiable credential while you're still building vocabulary. Skip it if you're already operationally fluent in Kubernetes and go straight to [CKA](/linux-foundation-coupon/cka/) instead, where the credential actually matches what you can already do.
